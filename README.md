## 🇬🇧 English

### 📖 Why this book matters

*The Machine That Changed the World* was published in 1990 by James P. Womack, Daniel T. Jones, and Daniel Roos. It distilled five years of research from MIT’s International Motor Vehicle Program (IMVP), a global study of how the automotive industry designs, builds, supplies, sells, and services products.

The book gave the world a practical name for a shift already visible at Toyota: **lean production**. Rather than treating the factory as an isolated efficiency problem, it compared three production systems across the entire value chain:

| System | Core strength | Core limitation |
|---|---|---|
| 🛠️ Craft production | Flexibility and customization | High cost; limited scale |
| 🏭 Mass production | Affordable, standardized output | Large batches, buffers, and slow change |
| ⚙️ Lean production | Quality, variety, speed, and efficient resource use | Requires disciplined learning across the whole system |

Its central insight remains useful far beyond manufacturing: better results come from connecting product development, operations, suppliers, customers, and management into one learning system. A local optimization that simply moves inventory, delays, defects, or risk elsewhere is not a real improvement.

### 🧭 What this skill helps with

`changing-the-world-machine` turns the book’s ideas into an on-demand reasoning aid for agents. Use it to:

- 🔎 Diagnose long lead times, high inventory, recurring defects, and handoff delays
- 🌊 Map and improve an end-to-end value stream
- 🧱 Design pull systems, smaller batches, and faster problem feedback
- 🤝 Build more capable supplier relationships instead of shifting cost and risk
- 🧩 Run cross-functional product development with earlier manufacturing and quality input
- 💬 Connect customer demand, delivery, service, and product learning
- 🌱 Guide a lean transformation as a sustained management practice — not a one-off tool rollout

### 🧠 How to get the most value

1. **Start with the customer-facing flow.** Ask what happens from demand to delivery, rather than beginning with a department chart.
2. **Make problems visible.** Treat inventory, inspection, expediting, and rework as signals to investigate — not permanent solutions.
3. **Solve at the source.** Use the skill’s root-cause and feedback-loop guidance before adding controls or buffers.
4. **Expand only after learning.** Validate changes in one complete, manageable value stream before scaling them.

## 🇨🇳 中文说明

### 📖 这本书为什么重要

《改变世界的机器：精益生产之道》由詹姆斯·P. 沃麦克、丹尼尔·T. 琼斯和丹尼尔·鲁斯于 1990 年出版。它凝结了麻省理工学院“国际汽车计划项目”（IMVP）长达五年的全球研究：研究团队考察汽车行业如何进行产品设计、制造、供应、销售和售后服务。

这本书为丰田等企业已在实践的系统化变革赋予了一个广为人知的名字：**精益生产**。它不把工厂仅仅看作局部效率问题，而是在完整价值链上比较三种生产方式：

| 生产方式 | 主要优势 | 主要局限 |
|---|---|---|
| 🛠️ 手工生产 | 高度灵活、可以定制 | 成本高，难以规模化 |
| 🏭 大批量生产 | 标准化、低单位成本 | 大批量、大缓冲、应对变化缓慢 |
| ⚙️ 精益生产 | 以更少资源实现质量、品种与速度 | 需要整个系统持续、严谨地学习 |

它最重要的启示不局限于制造业：产品开发、运营、供应商、客户和管理必须形成一个共同学习的系统。若所谓改善只是把库存、等待、缺陷或风险转移给别的部门、供应商或客户，就不是真正的改善。

### 🧭 这个 skill 能帮你做什么

`changing-the-world-machine` 将书中的精益思想组织成可按需调用的 Agent 推理工具，可用于：

- 🔎 诊断交付周期过长、库存过高、缺陷反复和部门交接等待
- 🌊 绘制并改善端到端价值流
- 🧱 设计拉动机制、小批量与更快的问题反馈
- 🤝 建立共同提升能力的供应商关系，而不是转嫁成本和风险
- 🧩 在产品开发早期连接设计、制造、质量与供应商
- 💬 将客户需求、交付、服务与产品学习形成闭环
- 🌱 把精益转型作为长期管理实践，而不是一次工具推广

### 🧠 如何获得最大价值

1. **从客户可感知的流程开始。** 先问从需求到交付发生了什么，而不是先看部门架构图。
2. **让问题显性化。** 将库存、检验、催单和返工视为需要追究的信号，而非永久解决方案。
3. **在源头解决问题。** 在新增控制或缓冲前，优先使用 skill 中的根因分析和反馈闭环方法。
4. **先学习，再扩展。** 先在一条完整、可控的价值流中验证，再复制到更大范围。
